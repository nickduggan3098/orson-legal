Orson Privacy Policy

Effective: September 29, 2026
Contact: nicholasdugganmain [at] gmail [dot] com

Orson is a speaking-practice app made by Nick Duggan ("we", "us"). This policy covers the TestFlight beta of Orson (version 1.0.0).

Your video never leaves your phone. Audio is shared only if you say yes.

What stays on your phone

  • Camera and microphone. Orson uses them only while you record a take, after you tap Spin.
  • Your video. Orson never uploads it. It plays on the Result screen, and it is deleted from Orson when you leave that screen. It leaves your phone only if you choose to save it to Photos or share it to TikTok or Instagram. What happens to it there is covered by those apps' policies.
  • Speech to text. Orson turns your speech into text with Apple's speech recognition, on your phone. Your audio is not sent to Apple or to us to do this. The first time, iOS may download Apple's language model; that download is a model, not your voice.
  • Your results and history. Your transcripts, scores, fillers, pace and pauses are stored on your phone. Deleting the app deletes them.

"Help improve Orson" (optional)

The first time you open Orson, it asks whether to share your takes. Nothing is sent unless you tap "share my takes". You can turn it off, or delete what you've shared, at any time on the Profile screen.

If you say yes, for each take after that we receive:

  • the take's audio (sound only, never video);
  • the transcript, with the time of each word;
  • the results: the fillers found and when, your pace, your pauses, and your score;
  • the prompt you were given;
  • the app version, your phone model (for example "iPhone 14") and your iOS version;
  • a device ID: a random ID made on your phone. It is not your name, email, Apple ID or an identifier from Apple.

Everything shared from your phone is linked to that device ID, so we can tell which takes came from the same phone and delete them all when you ask.

We don't receive your video, your name, your email, your contacts, your location, or any account.

What we use it for: analytics, only to make Orson better at hearing fillers and scoring speech. The device ID is also used for app functionality: it is how you delete what you've shared. We don't sell it, share it for advertising, or use it to track you.

How long we keep it: each shared take is deleted automatically 30 days after it is uploaded.

Deleting it sooner: Profile → "delete what I've shared", then tap again to confirm. This deletes every take shared from your phone. If you're offline, the request is kept and sent as soon as you're back online. Turning sharing off stops future takes from being sent, and drops any that were still waiting to send.

Where it's stored: with our database provider, Supabase, in the United States (Oregon), sent over an encrypted connection. The app's own key can only add a take or delete your takes; it cannot read anything back.

What Orson does not do

  • No accounts or sign-in in this beta.
  • No ads, no tracking, and no advertising, tracking, analytics or crash-reporting SDKs in the app.
  • No selling or renting of data.
  • As a TestFlight tester, Apple may send us crash reports and feedback that you choose to submit through TestFlight. Apple's privacy policy covers that.

Children

Orson isn't directed at children under 13, but anyone can use it. If you're under 13, please don't turn on sharing. If you believe a child has shared takes, email us and we'll delete them.

Changes

If this policy changes, we'll update the date above. The launch version of Orson will add accounts and subscriptions, and we'll update this policy before it ships.
